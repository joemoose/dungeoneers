# OSR Intro

## Introduction ==(long version draft)==

DUNGEONEERS is inspired by the early era of tabletop RPGs. If your experience is with more recent RPGs, some underlying assumptions may not apply, especially if you’ve played only modern Dungeons & Dragons.

###  Pillar 1: Rulings, Not Rules

Gameplay depends on GM rulings rather than strict procedures, with outcomes determined by a detailed rule set and required GM rolls. There are few rules about what you can do in the game. Instead, common sense, your current situation, and your decisions largely determine the results.

You describe any action your character attempts, and the GM describes the result, resorting to a quick die roll only when the outcome is uncertain, interesting, and relevant to the unfolding action. Otherwise, things generally happen as you describe. 

Most of your character abilities aren’t defined by rules because problem‑solving is meant to be hands‑on and guided by your approach to the problem. Taps, obstacles, and mysteries are overcome through observation, experimentation, and clear description. These events in the story aren’t decided by checking abilities on your character sheet or by relying on comprehensive rules that codify your interactions with the world around you.

Rules are generally tools for the GM, not necessarily player‑facing mechanics. A classic example is the pit trap: probe it with a 10’ pole to detect it. No specialized class abilities, no procedural mini‑game — just straightforward interaction with the world your character inhabits.

### Pillar 2: Player Skill, Not Character Abilities

Success depends on your ingenuity rather than on a list of character abilities, skills, or feats. There are no automatic checks rolled behind a GM screen to spot traps or detect lies. You must ask specific questions, describe exactly what your character examines, and articulate what you're telling NPCs.

Die rolls are the exception; careful thought is constant. Gameplay isn’t a simulation of how your character with a particular strength score would play out — it’s a series of challenges where your decisions keep the character alive.

### Pillar 3: Characters Are Not Superheroes

Your character is only slightly more capable than ordinary folks, and you survive through caution and cleverness. Even after many successful adventures, you gain renown, magic items, allies, and knowledge — not necessarily superhuman powers. You could defeat dragons through strategy, equipment, and spells, but never by overpowering them physically through ever-increasing abilities and stats from a specific character class.

The gameplay is firmly rooted in rising from humble beginnings to become a legendary hero, not in starting as a hero on the path to demigod status. In comic‑book terms, advancement leads toward Batman—resourceful, skilled, and formidable — not Superman. If the appeal lies in gritty growth rather than escalating superpowers, this style of play fits perfectly.

### Pillar 4: Forget Game Balance

Your adventures and campaigns will depict a living world, not a curated sequence of level‑appropriate encounters. You have no guarantee that every monster can be defeated, every trap disarmed, or every situation resolved by a rule or a dice roll. The game isn’t a competition between players and the GM; it’s a shared story shaped by your choices, your fellow players’ choices, and your GM’s adjudications.

### Summary

The GM describes the world; you decide how your character acts. The GM then describes the results, and you respond. Dice rolls are the exception, not the rule.

This conversation loop forms the foundation of DUNGEONEERS.

## ==Shorter Drafts==

### Draft 1

In DUNGEONEERS, you'll describe what your character does, and the GM makes quick rulings to keep the adventure moving. You’ll rely more on your own skill than on character abilities, poking around, asking questions, and trying clever ideas. The world isn’t balanced for you — danger is real, treasure is earned, and with a little luck and your own ingenuity, your hero grows from humble beginnings into something legendary.

### Draft 2

In DUNGEONEERS, you won't be consulting long lists of character abilities — you’ll be exploring, experimenting, and trusting your ingenuity as the GM makes quick rulings. Your character starts humble, gaining prowess through grit and a little luck rather than leveling up to gain new superhero powers. The world plays fair but not safely, so your decisions and ingenuity matter more than anything on your character sheet.

### Draft 3

Think of this game as stepping into a rough‑and‑tumble fantasy world where you describe your actions and the GM makes quick rulings to keep the adventure moving. You’ll solve problems with your ingenuity, not a list of character skills, and your hero will grow from a scrappy adventurer into a seasoned legend through your skill, not superpowers. The world isn’t balanced for you, so choose your risks wisely and enjoy the adventure.
