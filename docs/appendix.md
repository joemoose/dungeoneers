# Appendix

## A D&D Player's Guide to DUNGEONEERS

---

### TL;DR

*In DUNGEONEERS, you'll solve problems with your ingenuity, not your character sheet.*

---

### Inspiration

DUNGEONEERS is inspired by the early days of tabletop RPGs, a style of play known as the "Old School Renaissance." If you're familiar with modern Dungeons & Dragons, some of its conventions about how an RPG works may not apply. This guide highlights four foundational differences.

### Foundation 1: Rulings, Not Rules

Gameplay depends a lot on rulings from your GM rather than following strict game mechanics. There aren't many rules about what you can do or can't do or how you determine the results. Instead, the current situation, your decisions, and common sense largely determine the outcome of your actions.

You'll describe any action your character attempts, and the GM will describe the result. The GM will resort to a quick die roll only when the outcome is uncertain, risky, and meaningful to the unfolding story. Otherwise, things proceed as you describe them.

The outcomes of most actions aren't defined by rules because problem-solving is meant to be a hands-on aspect of the game and guided by your choices. Obstacles are overcome and mysteries resolved through observation, experimentation, and clear description. These challenges aren't solved by abilities on your character sheet or by relying on rules and dice rolls to resolve your interactions with the world.

The game's rules are not generally "player-facing," meaning things in the game world aren't passively discovered or resolved with dice rolls. A classic example is the pit trap: tap along the floor with a 10-foot pole to detect it. No specialized abilities or traits will find the trap for you. You need just straightforward interaction with your surroundings.

### Foundation 2: Player Skill, Not Character Abilities

Success depends more on *your* own ingenuity and resourcefulness than on your character. For example, the GM won't roll dice to check whether you spot a secret door or detect a lie. Instead, you'll have to ask the GM questions, describe what your character does, talk with an NPC, and listen to their response.

Die rolls happen, sure, but careful thought is constant. Gameplay isn't a simulation of how a character with a particular strength score, class, and level would play it out — it's a series of challenges where your fellow players' choices keep everyone alive. Knowing when to retreat is part of the game, and the world won't coddle you because of your level.

## Foundation 3: Characters Are Not Superheroes

Your character isn't much more capable than ordinary folks. You'll survive with caution and cleverness. Even after returning from a successful adventure, you probably gained renown, magic items, allies, and knowledge. Still, you won't simply increase numerical bonuses that eventually make you unstoppable.

For example, you can defeat dragons through strategy, equipment, and spells, but never by overpowering them through sheer character advancement. Standing toe-to-toe and trading blow for blow, whittling down HP, isn't going to work.

Gameplay is rooted in rising from humble beginnings to become a legendary hero, not in starting as a fledgling hero on the path to demigod status. In comic-book terms, advancement leads toward Batman — resourceful, clever, and equipped with the right gear — not Superman. If you find the appeal in gritty growth rather than escalating power curves, this style of play fits perfectly.

### Foundation 4: Forget Game Balance

Your adventures will take place in an ever-changing, living, breathing open world. The GM isn't going to present you with a sequence of level-appropriate encounters. There's no promise that every monster can be defeated and every obstacle overcome. You might have to fall back and try a new approach or take a different route. You won't always be able to beat everything. The game isn't a competition between players and the GM; it's a shared adventure story shaped by everyone's choices.

### Summary

The GM sets the scene; you describe how your character reacts. The GM then describes the outcomes, and you respond. Back and forth. Dice rolls are the exception, not the rule.

This dynamic loop is the foundation of DUNGEONEERS. Happy adventuring!
