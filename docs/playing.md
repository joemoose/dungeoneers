# Playing

## Introduction

The core of this game is a conversation between the players and the game master (GM). The GM describes the players’ current situation. Then, the players ask questions and tell the GM what their characters do. The GM explains the results of their actions, and then the players react again and describe more activities, and so on.

The GM answers your questions and always respects your freedom of choice. The GM provides challenges, obstacles, and situations to solve and overcome. Still, the game is more than just a story narrated by the GM—it’s an interactive narrative built on your and your fellow players’ decisions. Consider it as both creating and acting in an adventure story simultaneously.

Suppose the outcome of your character’s actions isn’t immediately apparent, and the activity is risky. In that case, the GM might ask you to roll dice to determine an outcome.

## Turns

The game uses turns to ensure everyone at the table has a chance to participate and speak. A turn is the time needed to move and act. The GM will go around the table and ask, “It’s your turn; what do you do?” 

Each player can move and take one action during their turn. A turn may represent several seconds (during combat), several minutes (exploring a room), several hours (exploring a city), or several days (traveling).

## Actions

An *action* is any task you attempt in the story. Your actions are typically aimed at achieving a goal or moving your adventure. The outcome of your action is *exactly* as you describe it unless it involves inherent *risk* or *uncertainty*. In that case, dice rolls determine the result.



!!! tip

	Dungeoneers are resourceful, if nothing else. Given sufficient time, some ability, and the proper equipment, your actions will generally succeed.
	
	Don't limit yourself to the actions in the manual. Describe what you want to accomplish, and the GM will determine whether a roll is required.

## Attribute Checks

Dungeoneers are resourceful, if nothing else. Given sufficient time, some ability, and the proper equipment, your actions will generally succeed. However, when you describe your character doing something risky with an essential but uncertain outcome, the GM may ask you to make an *attribute check* to determine whether you succeed. The GM will describe possible complications or outcomes to help you decide on your approach.

Roll **d20** and compare the result to the most relevant attribute score to determine an outcome as follows:

- If the roll is **equal to or less than** your attribute score, you succeed without complications.
- **If the roll is greater than** your attribute score, you fail and worsen things, creating an entirely new problem.



| Result | Outcome |
| - | - |
| **Less than** your attribute score | You succeed without complications.
| **Equal to** your attribute score | You succeed, but with a complication. You might have to make a trade-off, or the outcome might not be quite as good as you hoped.
| **Greater than** your attribute score | You fail and make things worse, creating an entirely new problem. |

!!! tip

	


## Attribute Saves

An action save determines the outcomes of all your risky actions in the game. 

==Saves==
==A save is a roll to avoid negative outcomes from risky choices. Characters roll a d20 and compare the results to the appropriate attribute. If they roll equal to or under that attribute, they succeed. Otherwise, they fail. A 1 is always a success, and a 20 is always a failure.==
==If two opponents are each trying to overcome the other, whoever is most at risk should save.==
==If two characters need to take an action together, whoever is most at risk should save (usually the character with the lowest relevant Attribute).==
